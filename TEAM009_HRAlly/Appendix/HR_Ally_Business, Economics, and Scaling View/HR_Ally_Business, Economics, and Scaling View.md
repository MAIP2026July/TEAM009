# 1. Business, Economics, and Scaling View

*HR Ally – An AI-Powered Assistant for Employee HR Support*

> **Status:** Revised analytical draft for TEAM009 integration, aligned to the HR Ally Product Proposal, the Product Requirements Document (PRD) and the Data, Model and Evaluation Strategy. All financial numbers below are illustrative assumptions for a hypothetical enterprise, not findings from actual HR operations or vendor quotations.

## 1.1 Business model and value proposition

HR Ally is an internal enterprise HR self-service capability delivered through two channels on one shared back end: a Microsoft Teams integration and an intranet web portal for employees without Teams access (PRD FR-13, FR-14). Its purpose is to improve productivity and employee experience, not to generate direct subscription revenue. The organization bears the cost of development, AI inference, search infrastructure, HR content maintenance, evaluation, operational support and governance; value is generated by faster employee access to accurate, source-backed policy guidance and fewer repetitive HR-desk inquiries.

**Product principle (from the proposal):** HR Ally reduces repetitive work; it does not replace HR professionals. The economic case therefore rests on redirected HR capacity and recovered employee time, not on headcount reduction.

The first wedge is limited to source-grounded questions on routine policies and benefits, including leave, attendance, expenses and payroll/salary-document guidance. The assistant gives a concise answer, a source reference and a next step, but does not approve leave, determine eligibility, change records or decide sensitive matters; grievances, disciplinary concerns, employee relations, compensation decisions and policy exceptions go directly to qualified HR staff (PRD H1, H3, H7). In line with the proposal’s build vs buy view, HR Ally uses an approved AI platform or API rather than a custom-trained model, so investment goes into authoritative content, guided workflows, access control, escalation and evaluation rather than model training.

## 1.2 Measurable value logic

Each value lever is tied to a PRD goal and success-evidence measure, so the business case can be tested with the same data used to evaluate the product.

| Value lever | PRD goal | Success evidence and measure | Economic treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Employee efficiency | G1 faster answers; G5 correct next step | SE-1 time to answer vs manual process; SE-5 share routed to the right form, portal or channel | Employee capacity value (productivity, not cash) |
| HR-team capacity | G4 fewer repetitive questions | SE-4 reduction in covered HR inquiries (pilot stage) | HR capacity value; cash only if staffing or overtime costs actually fall |
| Service quality and trust | G2, G3, G6 | SE-2 grounded answers ≥95%; SE-3 rating ≥4.0/5; SE-6 fewer clarifications; SE-8 critical errors \<1% | Not monetised; a precondition for adoption, which drives both values above |
| Safe escalation | G7 | SE-7 ≥90% correct escalation; zero sensitive questions answered by AI | Risk avoidance; escalated cases remain HR work and are not counted as avoided inquiries |
| Channel reach | G8 | Every measure reported separately for Teams and the portal | Portal extends value to non-Teams employees at a small marginal cost on the shared back end |

- Not all saved time converts to cash: time recovered is a productivity or capacity benefit unless staffing, overtime or other costs actually fall. Cash savings must be evidenced separately.

- Clarifications and escalations still consume time: only questions resolved without HR involvement count toward avoided inquiries.

Non-AI comparator: current HR portal, policy search, FAQ pages and human help-desk service. Following PRD SE-1, the same question set is answered through HR Ally and through the existing process, so the pilot tests whether HR Ally improves time-to-answer and quality enough to justify its incremental running costs, rather than assuming generative AI is always superior.

## 1.3 Cost structure

| Cost component | How it scales / why it matters | When incurred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI model and embeddings | Variable with requests, token volume, chosen model and retrieval design. The model is selected as the cheapest candidate that meets every evaluation target. | Prototype onward; variable |
| Knowledge retrieval and cloud services | Hybrid search index, hosting, databases, storage, monitoring and availability. | Prototype onward |
| Channels: Teams app and intranet portal | Teams app approval, portal hosting, single sign-on. One shared back end keeps the second channel’s marginal cost low. | One-time setup, then run cost |
| HR policy content operations | Document approval, metadata, freshness review, effective dates, routing-catalogue link checks, sensitive-topic taxonomy. | Ongoing, per content owner |
| Evaluation and human review | Building the 300–500 question test set, HR reviewer scoring (two reviewers, ≥150 answers per release), red-teaming and regression runs. | Each release |
| Escalation handling | HR time on escalated, unclear and sensitive cases, which remain human work by design. | Ongoing; scales with usage |
| Security, governance and audit | Access reviews, log retention, incident response and auditability (PRD non-functional requirements). | Ongoing |
| Integration (later release) | Optional HR system connectors and request workflows (PRD FR-16). | Stage 4 only |

Pricing and licensing must be validated with approved enterprise vendors before any budget approval. Existing Microsoft Teams licences should not be assumed to cover all incremental AI services, and intranet portal hosting must be costed separately. Evaluation effort is a real recurring cost: for example, two reviewers scoring 150 answers at an assumed 3 minutes each is about 15 HR hours, or roughly INR 9,000 per release at the illustrative rate below.

## 1.4 Illustrative unit-economics scenario (not actual results)

To show the calculation method, the following example models an organization already using Microsoft Teams and an intranet. It is a planning scenario only; all input figures require baseline validation during the prototype and evaluation stages.

| Assumption | Illustrative value | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employees | 5,000 | Hypothetical organization size |
| Eligible HR Ally questions/month | 2,500 | Illustrative in-scope interactions across both channels |
| Baseline covered HR-desk inquiries/month | 1,500 | Separate baseline help-desk demand in selected categories |
| Pilot self-service resolution rate | 50% | Proposed pilot target in PM Artifact Pack |
| Reduction in covered HR-desk inquiries | 20% | Proposed target, PRD SE-4 and PM Artifact Pack |
| HR handling time / inquiry | 8 minutes | Assumed current average handling time |
| Employee time saved / resolved question | 5 minutes | Assumed improvement versus existing search/process (SE-1) |
| HR capacity value / hour | INR 600 | Illustrative loaded hourly value |
| Employee capacity value / hour | INR 450 | Illustrative loaded hourly value |
| Monthly total operating cost | INR 50,000 | Illustrative combined technology, content, evaluation, support and governance |
| One-time build/onboarding cost | INR 300,000 | Illustrative engineering, two-channel integration and test-set build |

Computation (steady state): 1,500 × 20% = 300 HR-desk inquiries avoided/month; 300 × 8 / 60 = 40 HR hours; 40 × INR 600 = INR 24,000/month of HR capacity value. Separately, 2,500 × 50% = 1,250 self-service resolutions; 1,250 × 5 / 60 × INR 450 = INR 46,875/month of employee time value. These are different labor pools and should be tracked separately.

| Monthly output | Illustrative amount |
| --- | --- |
| HR capacity value | INR 24,000 |
| Employee time value | INR 46,875 |
| Combined potential productivity value | INR 70,875 |
| Recurring operating cost | INR 50,000 |
| Net potential productivity value | INR 20,875 |
| Cost per assistant interaction | INR 20.00 |
| Cost per self-service resolution | INR 40.00 |
| Cost per avoided HR-desk inquiry | INR 166.67 |

Under these assumptions, simple payback on the INR 300,000 initial investment is approximately 14.4 months (300,000 / 20,875), excluding ramp-up, financing, taxes and any additional implementation costs. This is not a forecast or guaranteed return. The HR-capacity benefit alone (INR 24,000/month) does not cover the INR 50,000/month operating cost; the financial case depends on credible employee productivity value or additional measurable benefits.

## 1.5 Sensitivity, break-even and decision implications

| Case | HR inquiry reduction / self-service resolution | Gross productivity value / month | Value net of INR 50k OPEX |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative | 10% / 30% | INR 40,125 | \- INR 9,875 |
| Base (pilot targets) | 20% / 50% | INR 70,875 | INR 20,875 |
| Expansion (illustrative) | 30% / 65% | INR 96,938 | INR 46,938 |

**Break-even.** Holding the HR-desk reduction at the 20% target, employee time value must cover the remaining INR 26,000/month. At INR 37.50 per resolution (5 / 60 × 450), that needs about 694 resolutions, a self-service resolution rate of roughly 28% of 2,500 questions. Relying on HR capacity alone would need 625 avoided inquiries a month (50,000 / INR 80 per inquiry), about 42% of the covered baseline and twice the proposed target.

The sensitivity shows why HR Ally should be piloted before enterprise rollout: weak adoption or lower-than-expected inquiry reduction can erase the estimated benefits, and the case leans on employee time saved, so the pilot must measure SE-1 as rigorously as SE-4. These scenarios are model inputs, not measured outputs. A finance decision should account for incremental cash costs, recoverable labor capacity, employee outcome improvements and whether future savings can actually be realized.

## 1.6 Operating model and scaling plan

The scaling plan follows the PRD release approach (prototype, evaluate, limited pilot, later releases); each stage re-runs the unit economics with better evidence. Month ranges are indicative.

| Stage | Scope and activities | Economic focus | Gate to move on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Prototype (0–2 months) | Narrow prototype in Teams and the portal showing all six MVP elements for selected routine topics; knowledge base and first 300 test questions. | Baseline the manual process: time to answer, inquiry volumes, handling time; confirm vendor pricing. | All six MVP elements work end to end in both channels |
| 2. Evaluate (2–3 months) | Offline evaluation on 300–500 HR-reviewed questions, red-teaming, model selection, prompt and threshold tuning. | Measured cost per question and latency; re-run 1.4 with measured inputs. | SE-1 to SE-3 and SE-5 to SE-8 met in both channels |
| 3. Limited pilot (4–6 months) | Controlled use by a representative employee group in both channels; weekly content review and monitoring. | Measure SE-4, real resolution and time saved; separate cash from capacity value. | Pilot gates in 1.7 met; no critical incidents |
| 4. Later releases (7–12 months) | More topics and languages; limited personalization once access control is proven; governed HR workflow integrations (FR-16). | Recalculate cost at higher usage; separate business case for each integration. | Pilot evidence and governance approval |

| Operating role | Responsibility | Recurring effort driver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HR content owners | Approve, maintain and expire policy sources (PRD H6) | Number of policies and change frequency |
| HR support channel | Receive escalations and sensitive cases with conversation context | Escalation volume and service-level target |
| HR reviewers | Score sampled answers, review negative feedback, sign off releases | Release cadence and sample size |
| Platform team | Run the shared back end, both channels, monitoring and cost alerts | Usage volume and incident rate |

## 1.7 Scaling constraints and go/no-go criteria

| Constraint | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Answer correctness / policy drift | Approved-source retrieval, effective dates, HR ownership, regression testing and escalation. |
| Privacy and permissions | Identity checks, least-privilege retrieval, access enforcement, minimization and incident handling. |
| Latency and cost growth | Load-test peak demand, track tokens and cost/query, use model routing and cost alerts. |
| Human escalation capacity | Measure escalation rate and queue SLA; size HR review capacity before expansion. |
| Adoption and proof of value | Compare with traditional FAQ/search; measure avoided inquiries and employee effort. |
| Channel parity | One shared back end; every metric reported per channel so Teams and portal quality cannot drift apart (PRD R8). |
| Vendor and integration dependence | Approved APIs, documented service limits, fallback route and change management. |

| Proposed pilot gate | Threshold | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grounded answers (SE-2) | ≥95% | PRD, proposed |
| Helpful and understandable (SE-3) | ≥4.0 / 5 | PRD, proposed |
| Covered HR-desk inquiry reduction (SE-4) | ≥20% | PRD and PM Artifact Pack |
| Self-service resolution rate | ≥50% | PM Artifact Pack |
| Safe escalation (SE-7) | ≥90%; zero sensitive answered by AI | PRD, proposed |
| Critical policy errors (SE-8) | \<1% | PRD, proposed |
| p95 response time | \<8 seconds | PM Artifact Pack |
| Unauthorized data exposure | Zero confirmed incidents | PM Artifact Pack; PRD H5 |
| Channel parity (G8) | No material gap between Teams and portal | PRD |

All thresholds are subject to validation after baselining. A severe employee-impacting policy error must trigger review irrespective of aggregate success rates.

## 1.8 Risks to the business case

The proposal’s main risks also carry economic consequences; each is controlled by a product safeguard and checked by a pilot gate.

| Risk (PRD ref.) | Economic impact | Control and check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incorrect, outdated or unsupported answers (R1, R2) | Rework, wrong form use and lost trust reduce adoption and resolution rate | Approved sources, citations, answerability check; SE-2 and SE-8 gates |
| Personal data exposure (R3) | Incident and compliance costs could outweigh all productivity value | SSO and authorization before personal data; zero-incident gate |
| Misread dates or details (R4) | Extra clarification and correction effort | Clarification step; employee reviews before submission; SE-6 |
| Sensitive matters mishandled (R5) | Employee-relations harm and reputational cost | Sensitivity check routes to HR first; zero sensitive answered |
| Over-trust and loss of personal touch (R6, R7) | Wrong actions taken on guidance; low adoption if employees feel unheard | Guidance labelling; escalation to a person always offered; SE-3 |

## 1.9 Recommendation

Proceed with a narrow, source-grounded prototype in both channels, then offline evaluation and a monitored internal pilot, not a full-scale enterprise rollout. Confirm the HR baseline and vendor pricing, test the non-AI alternative, collect reliable HR-reviewed quality and resolution data, and re-run the unit-economics model at each stage before deciding whether to expand. Product scope should remain evidence-led: trusted HR guidance first, transactional automation only after security, governance, operational capacity and business value are demonstrated.
