## 4. AI weaknesses and human corrections

| Issue | How it was detected | Human correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The worked evaluation example miscounted its checks ("twelve of fifteen" passed instead of eleven of fourteen) | Consistency check of the example | Count corrected |
| The test plan introduction said seven test types while the table listed eight | Consistency check | Text corrected |
| The evaluation set grew from 300–400 to 680 items, out of step with earlier documents | Cross-document comparison | A note in the strategy explains the larger set (enough sensitive and adversarial items for meaningful results) |
| Some regulatory references (POSH Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act) came from general knowledge rather than cited sources | Review of the note's sources | Marked as such in the note; data-protection statements tied to cited sources |
| Risk IDs overlapped in crowded cells of the heat map | Visual check of the rendered chart | Cell widths increased and the chart redrawn |

**Controls on known limitations:**

- **Financial inputs:** Section 9 figures are illustrative scenarios and are never described as measured ROI; cash savings are kept separate from capacity released.
- **Plans versus deliverables:** all sections were cross-checked against the professor's packet; the faculty requires a functional MVP, which the team's prototype and Teams integration demo address.
- **Policy answers:** the design requires approved-source citations, uncertainty handling and escalation to authorised HR, and the evaluation plan tests each of these.
- **Attribution:** each member is responsible for the AI-assisted content in their own deliverables.

## 5. Human ownership and checks

The team retains responsibility for opportunity choice, MVP scope, feasibility, prioritisation, risk acceptance, monetisation, evaluation thresholds and go/no-go decisions. It cross-checks artifacts against the professor's packet and the Product Proposal, validates calculations, and does not submit raw AI output unreviewed.

## 6. Team reflection

AI tools accelerated structure, drafting, alternatives and consistency checks. Their main weaknesses were plausible but unvalidated numbers and small internal inconsistencies, which human review caught and corrected. AI outputs are not proof of viability, financial return, correct HR answers, working integrations or safe operation; verified assumptions, HR-approved sources, testing and human judgment remain essential before a pilot.
